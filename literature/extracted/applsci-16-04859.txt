Abstract
The Agricultural Energy Internet (AEI) represents an emerging systemic paradigm driven
by the convergence of intelligent agriculture and rural energy transformation. It is not a
simple extension of agricultural informatization or rural electrification; rather, it redefines
agricultural processes—such as irrigation, greenhouse environmental control, supplemen-
tary lighting, cold-chain logistics, and agricultural machinery—as perceptible, computable,
and schedulable energy-related processes, thereby enabling the deep integration of agricul-
ture, energy, environmental management, and intelligent decision-making. This review
systematically examines the evolutionary trajectory of AEI, from early agricultural digi-
talization and Internet of Things (IoT)-based monitoring to edge intelligence and digital
twin technologies, and ultimately to the coordinated optimization of agriculture–energy–
environment systems. A comprehensive technical framework is established, encompassing
physical energy coupling, multi-source sensing and actuation, interconnection and in-
teroperability, edge–cloud collaborative control, data governance, digital twin modeling,
artificial intelligence-enabled optimization, and application-oriented decision-making. The
review further highlights that high-quality data governance, edge–cloud collaboration, and
digital twin calibration are critical enablers of the transition from visualization-oriented
management to closed-loop intelligent operation. In addition, this study clarifies the com-
plementary relationship between agricultural informatization and electrification: the former
provides capabilities for perception, prediction, optimization, and coordination, whereas
the latter provides a controllable execution chain. Together, they constitute the foundation
of a cyber-physical agricultural energy system. Finally, frontier research directions are
identified, including high-temperature solid oxide electrolysis for hydrogen production,
edge AI–IoT-enabled closed-loop agricultural operation, and privacy, security, and trust
mechanisms in federated edge intelligence. The findings suggest that AEI can serve as a
strategic technological framework for supporting the next generation of smart agriculture
toward low-carbon, resilient, and collaborative operation.
Keywords: Agricultural Energy Internet; smart agriculture; agricultural electrification;
collaborative optimization; artificial intelligence

applications across the agricultural sector. They invested USD 5 billion to deploy Internet of
Things devices on farms, develop artificial intelligence models, and realize cross-state data
sharing. Their goal is to achieve 90% coverage of precision agriculture in major corn and
soybean production areas by 2026 [4]. In 2022, Germany’s Federal Ministry of Food and
Agriculture introduced the Smart Agriculture 2030 initiative, focusing on sensor networks,
autonomous agricultural machinery [ 5], and carbon footprint monitoring technologies,
while encouraging SMEs to participate through tax incentives and subsidies.
Clean and efficient use of agricultural energy is the cornerstone of agricultural policies
in the United States and Europe. The European Union unveiled its “Rural Renewable
Energy Action Plan” in 2024, integrating agricultural energy transition into the framework
of the “European Green Deal” and setting a target for renewable energy to account for
50% of agricultural energy consumption by 2030. The plan prioritizes supporting farm
photovoltaic (PV) power stations, biomass energy utilization, and smart microgrids, while
offering 30% investment subsidies for rural distributed PV projects [6]. The U.S. Inflation
Reduction Act of 2023 allocated $3.5 billion specifically for agricultural energy transition,
including subsidies for purchasing clean energy equipment on farms, tax credits for re-
newable energy generation, and rural grid upgrades, encouraging farmers to adopt PV
energy storage systems for energy self-sufficiency [ 7]. In 2023, the UK Department of
Energy launched the “Rural Clean Energy Plan”, investing £2 billion to support wind and
PV projects in agricultural parks and promote flexible interconnection between agricultural
energy systems and the main grid [8], with the aim to achieve 80% clean energy coverage
in rural areas by 2025.
Currently, the advancement of intelligent agriculture in China has moved into the
stage of extensive promotion and nationwide deployment, becoming a core pillar of rural
revitalization and agricultural modernization. Data from the Ministry of Agriculture
and Rural Affairs (MARA) shows that the total investment in China’s smart agriculture
sector topped 120 billion yuan over the 2022–2024 period. Emerging technologies like the
Internet of Things, big data, and AI have been fully incorporated into the entire lifecycle
of staple crops, including wheat, rice, and maize, spanning sowing [9], cultivation, field
management, and crop harvesting.

Guidelines were released by the National Rural Revitalization Administration (NRRA).
This policy regards smart agriculture as a core element of digital rural development. It
also encourages the construction of digital agricultural service stations in rural regions [12].
These measures aim to enhance farmers’ digital application skills.
The transition of agricultural energy is an important starting point for China’s rural
energy revolution and green agricultural development, with relevant policies mainly
focusing on clean energy substitution and efficient utilization. Released in 2024, the
2024–2027 Implementation Plan for Rural Energy Revitalization was officially promulgated
by the National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) together with the National
Energy Administration (NEA) in a joint effort. The plan clearly sets a target for 2027. By
that year [13], the share of renewable energy consumption in rural regions is projected to
reach 25 percent.
Key priorities of this scheme include county-scale distributed PV expansion, integrated
exploitation of rural biomass energy, and intelligent microgrid development in agricultural
parks, alongside kWh subsidies for rural PV and energy storage constructions. The National
Energy Administration jointly initiated the Rural Energy Comprehensive Demonstration
Project with the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs in 2023. The project promoted
technologies such as PV-equipped greenhouses, livestock manure biogas production, and
agricultural machinery electrification in 200 counties across the country. Up to now, more
than 300,000 rural distributed PV projects have been established [14]. Their total installed
capacity has exceeded 150 million kilowatts.
In 2022, the NEA released the document “Guidance on Accelerating the Transforma-
tion and Development of Rural Energy”. The document explicitly supports clean energy
alternatives for agricultural production. It also encourages integrated models [ 14], such
as agricultural PV and wind-power complementarity. In addition, it promotes the deep
integration of rural energy systems with agricultural production.
Globally, smart agriculture has become a core direction of agricultural transformation.
Countries around the world have promoted their development through policy guidance,
capital investment, and technological innovation, while the clean and efficient utilization
of agricultural energy has become an important supporting trend. Keeping pace with

1.2. Review Methodology and Analytical Criteria
To improve the transparency of the research methodology, this review divides the
literature review process into four consecutive stages: literature identification, preliminary
screening, eligibility assessment, and analytical coding. The academic databases used
for formal retrieval include the Web of Science Core Collection, Scopus, IEEE Xplore,
ScienceDirect, and SpringerLink. Google Scholar is only adopted for forward and backward
citation tracking, as well as the supplementary retrieval of early basic research. In addition,
this paper cites authoritative policy documents and statistical reports released by the
Food and Agriculture Organization, the Organisation for Economic Co-Operation and
Development, the European Commission, the United States Department of Agriculture,
the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs, the National Development and Reform
Commission, and the National Energy Administration. These materials are only used as
background references and not included in the core technical evidence. Within the scope of
database functions, the retrieval covers titles, abstracts, and keywords. A total of more than
170 literature records were obtained through initial retrieval and citation tracking. After
deduplication, title and abstract screening, and full-text eligibility assessment, 143 studies
were finally retained as the core technical literature, with policy documents supplemented
as contextual background materials.
The final formal database retrieval and citation-tracking update were completed on
30 April 2026. For reproducibility, the core Boolean search strings were applied to titles,
abstracts, and keywords where supported by each database:
(S1) (“Agricultural Energy Internet” OR “rural energy internet” OR “agricultural
energy system” OR “farm energy system”) AND (“smart agriculture” OR “precision
agriculture” OR “agricultural IoT” OR “digital agriculture”).
(S2) (“agricultural electrification” OR “electric agricultural machinery” OR “green-
house load” OR irrigation OR “cold chain”) AND (photovoltaic OR renewable OR micro-
grid OR “energy storage” OR “demand response”).
(S3) (“agricultural digital twin” OR “edge computing” OR “edge intelligence” OR
“AIoT” OR “closed-loop control”) AND (greenhouse OR irrigation OR livestock OR farm).
(S4) (“agricultural microgrid” OR “rural microgrid” OR “carbon-constrained dispatch”
OR “virtual power plant”) AND (agriculture OR greenhouse OR irrigation OR rural). Equiv-
alent syntax adjustments were made only when a database required different wildcard or
quotation rules, while the logical meaning of each query was kept unchanged.
