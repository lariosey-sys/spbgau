Abstract 
 
Greenhouse climate control presents a complex challenge involv ing 
nonlinear dynamics, environmental uncertainty, and the need for 
economically efficient decisions. This paper proposes a novel hybrid 
control framework —RL-Guided MPC —that integrates Reinforcement 
Learning (RL) with Model Predictive Control (MPC) to enha nce 
economic performance in autonomous greenhouse management. The 
approach uses a trained Soft Actor -Critic RL agent to define both a 
terminal cost and terminal region constraint, thereby guiding the MPC’s 
decision-making without requiring long prediction horizons. 
Simulations were conducted under both deterministic and stochastic 
conditions using a validated greenhouse model. Comparative 
evaluations with standalone MPC and RL controllers reveal that RL -
Guided MPC consistently outperforms both, especially a t shorter 
horizons, by effectively balancing long -term economic objectives with 
short-term system constraints. The framework retains MPC’s strengths 
in constraint satisfaction while leveraging RL’s ability to learn optimal 
control strategies from experienc e. While the RL agent alone 
underperformed in cumulative reward metrics due to constraint 
handling limitations, its integration into MPC notably improved overall 
system performance. These results demonstrate the potential of 
combining data -driven policies with model -based control for energy -
efficient, economically optimal greenhouse operation.

1 Introduction 
Greenhouses play a crucial role in sustainable agriculture by providing a controlled environment that 
enhances crop yield while promoting efficient resource usage. To manage these systems effectively, 
especially under varying external weather conditions, advanced control methods are needed to 
dynamically regulate key climate variables such as temperature, humidity, and CO ₂  concentration. 
However, traditional control techniques often fall short when it comes to balancing real -time 
operational decisions with long-term economic performance. 
Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Model Predictive Control (MPC) have both emerged as promising 
tools to tackle these challenges. RL is well -suited for dealing with uncertainty and learning optimal 
strategies through experience, while MPC off ers a robust, model -based approach that enforces 
constraints and adapts decisions over time. Previous studies have largely investigated RL and MPC 
independently. For instance, van Laatum et al. (2024) introduced an open -source environment to 
evaluate vario us RL methods for greenhouse control, and Morcego et al. (2023) developed a deep 
deterministic policy gradient-based RL controller and compared its performance with that of an MPC -
based solution. On the MPC side, Boersma et al. (2022) proposed a robust MPC  controller that 
improved upon traditional implementations. Yazdi et al. (2024) propose a hybrid control strategy that 
combines MPC with reinforcement learning to enhance quadruped locomotion in dynamic 
environments. Their results show improved velocity tr acking and task efficiency using the RLMPC 
approach compared to pure MPC.  A more integrated approach was explored by Mallick et al. (2025), 
where MPC was used as a function approximator within an RL framework. 
The core of the greenhouse control problem lie s in economic optimization. While conventional MPC 
typically targets steady -state tracking of economically favorable conditions, such static approaches 
may not suit greenhouse operations, which benefit more from time -varying adjustments to climate 
conditions for better economic returns. Economic MPC, which directly incorporates economic costs 
into the control objective rather than focusing on stabilizing to a fixed reference, is a better fit. Still, 
many implementations of economic MPC rely on steady -state or reference trajectories to ensure 
stability and performance (as discussed in Amrit et al., 2011; Risbeck and Rawlings, 2020; Angeli et 
al., 2012). 
This paper proposes a novel hybrid approach—RL-Guided MPC—that leverages a trained RL policy 
to enhance the  MPC framework. Specifically, the RL policy is used to define both the terminal cost, 
via a cost function approximator, and the terminal region constraint, based on policy rollouts. This 
combination enables the controller to maintain high performance even with shorter prediction 
horizons, improving computational efficiency without compromising control quality. 
To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed method, simulations were carried out in two settings: a 
deterministic environment and one with parametr ic uncertainty. The RL -Guided MPC controller is 
compared against traditional MPC and standalone RL in both cases. 
The remainder of the paper is organized as follows: Section 2 introduces the greenhouse model, 
simulation scenarios, and the shared optimizati on problem. Sections 3 and 4 detail the RL agent and 
MPC controller, respectively. Section 5 presents the proposed RL -Guided MPC framework. Section 6 
provides numerical results, and conclusions are drawn in Section 7.

2.2 Optimization Problem 
The objective of optimal greenhouse control is to sustain environmental conditions that promote 
healthy crop development —specifically regulating temperature, humidity, and CO 2 concentration—
while keeping resource usage to a minimum. This regulation is performed by adjusting control inputs: 
heating (via pipe systems), ventilation (through window operation),  and CO 2 enrichment (via 
injection). The overarching goal is to maximize crop yield while minimizing input costs and energy 
consumption.
